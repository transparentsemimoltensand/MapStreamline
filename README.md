# Mumbai Road Network: Spatial Analysis Brief

## Overview
This project analyzes the structural topology of Mumbai’s road network using spatial data from **Overture Maps**, **OpenStreetMap (OSM)**, and **municipal boundary datasets**. It explores how physical constraints shape urban mobility, identifies gaps in open mapping data, and evaluates road topology.

---

## Core Findings

### 1. Physical Geography & Corridor Bottlenecks
* Mumbai’s narrow peninsula geography forces a distinct **north-south linear transit spine**.
* High coastal density and natural water bodies constrain east-west expansion, channeling primary highways into tight spatial corridors with limited alternative routing.

### 2. OSM Data Gaps & The Industrial Paradox
* While restricted industrial zones explain missing local or service roads, they do not justify missing **secondary and tertiary connections**.
* Unclassified or secondary roads supporting industrial freight are frequently omitted by community mappers who prioritize residential areas, creating false disconnects in open-source network graphs.

### 3. Resolving "Open Loops" with Tertiary Connectors
* Macro-level highway loops often appear incomplete or "open" when looking strictly at primary road datasets.
* In reality, **localized tertiary and secondary road grids** close these loops, organically stitching high-speed expressways into surrounding neighborhood networks.
